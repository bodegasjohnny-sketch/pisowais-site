Loans 101, free lesson

Why Filipinos borrow and how loans actually work

Utang is not a bad word. Utang na hindi mo naiintindihan, yun ang delikado.

Halos lahat tayo may utang at some point: tuition, motor, puhunan, hospital bill. This guide explains why people borrow, what you are really paying for, and how to tell a helpful loan from a heavy one.

The 4 common reasons people borrow

Most borrowing falls into four buckets. Survival: food, rent and fare when income is short. Emergency: hospital, accident, repairs. Asset: a house, a motor for work, a laptop for freelancing. Business: puhunan for stock, equipment or a bigger order.

The first two usually mean the budget or the buffer has a gap. The last two can be useful, if the thing you buy helps you earn or saves you money over time.

Example Aling Nena runs a sari-sari store in Bulacan. She borrows to buy stock in bulk before Christmas because the supplier gives a lower price per case. That is a business loan with a clear plan to pay it back from sales.

What a loan really is

A loan is simple: someone gives you money now, and you agree to pay it back later, plus a price for the time you used it. That price is interest. There may also be fees, like a processing fee or a late penalty.

Every loan has five parts you should be able to name: principal (the amount borrowed), interest rate, term (how long), payment schedule, and fees. If you cannot name all five, you are not ready to sign.

Good debt vs heavy debt, a simple test

Ask two questions. First: will this loan help me earn or save more than it costs? Second: can I pay the monthly from my normal income without borrowing again?

Two yes answers is a loan that can work for you. One no is a warning. Two no answers usually means the loan will grow into a cycle.

Example only, not a real offer. Jun, a delivery rider in Cebu, compares two choices for a second-hand motor. Option A: borrow and pay a fixed amount monthly from his rider income, which he has tracked for 6 months. Option B: borrow for a new phone on installment because the old one is "luma na". A passes both questions. B passes neither.

Where people borrow from

Common sources are banks, lending and financing companies, cooperatives, government programs like SSS or Pag-IBIG loans for members, credit cards, buy-now-pay-later apps, and family or friends. Each has different costs, rules and risks.

For any lending company, check that it is registered. Under RA 9474, no lending company shall conduct business unless granted an authority to operate by the SEC. Search the company name on the SEC website before you apply.

5

Lesson 5 Free

Before you borrow, try these first

Can the need wait two weeks while you save? Can you sell something you do not use? Can you ask for a payment plan directly from the hospital, school or supplier? Sometimes the cheapest loan is the one you did not need.

If you do borrow, borrow the exact amount, for a clear purpose, with a written plan for every payment.

FAQ

Is it bad to have utang?

Not by itself. A planned loan for an asset or business can help. The risk is borrowing for daily needs month after month, or not knowing the total cost.

How do I know a lender is legit?

For lending and financing companies, check registration and authority to operate on the SEC website. For banks, check that it is BSP-supervised. If unsure, do not proceed.

Should I borrow to lend to relatives?

Avoid it. You carry the full obligation even if they do not pay you back.
