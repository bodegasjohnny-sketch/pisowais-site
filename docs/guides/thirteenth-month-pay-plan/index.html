13th month pay plan: before it arrives, decide where it goes

Dumating ang 13th month, nawala sa Noche Buena at sale. Sound familiar?

The 13th month pay is a legal benefit for many private-sector rank-and-file employees. Here is how it is computed, when it is due, and a simple plan to make it last past January.

Lesson 1 Free

The rules, briefly

Memorandum Order No. 28 (1986) requires employers to pay rank-and-file employees a 13th month pay not later than December 24 every year. The PD 851 implementing rules define it as one-twelfth (1/12) of the basic salary earned within the calendar year. Allowances and benefits not part of basic salary are generally not included. Rules have details and exceptions, so check DOLE's current guidance for your situation.

2

Lesson 2 Free

Compute your estimate

Add up the basic salary you earned this calendar year and divide by 12. If you joined mid-year, use only the months you worked.

Example only. Basic salary ₱20,000 a month, worked all 12 months: 20,000 × 12 / 12 = ₱20,000. Worked 7 months: 20,000 × 7 / 12 = ₱11,666.67.

Lesson 3 Free

The tax part

Under the TRAIN law (RA 10963), 13th month pay and other benefits are exempt from income tax up to a combined ₱90,000 per year (sourced, see below). Amounts above that are taxable.

4

Lesson 4 Free

A simple split before it lands

Decide the split before payday so it does not disappear. One option: clear upcoming debts first, top up your emergency fund, set aside January expenses like tuition or renewals, then enjoy the rest guilt-free.

Example only. ₱20,000: ₱6,000 to pay off a card balance, ₱6,000 to emergency fund, ₱4,000 reserved for January bills, ₱4,000 for Christmas.

5

Lesson 5 Free

January is part of the plan

Many people forget that December card swipes become January bills. Set aside that amount now.

6

Lesson 6 Free

If you are an employer

Small business owners should set aside 13th month money monthly, about one-twelfth of each month's basic payroll, so December does not become a cash flow gap. Our guide on cash flow gaps explains how to plan lumpy expenses.

FAQ

I am a freelancer or commission-based. Do I get it?

Coverage depends on the type of work and pay. Some exclusions exist in the rules. Check DOLE guidance or ask your employer or HR.

Can it be paid in two parts?

Some employers split it. The deadline for the full amount is December 24.

Is the calculator exact?

It is an estimate of the minimum. Your employer may pay more.
