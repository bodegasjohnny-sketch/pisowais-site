Lesson 1 Free

Amortization in one sentence

An amortization is a fixed regular payment that covers that month's interest first, and the rest goes to the principal. Early on, more of your payment goes to interest. Near the end, almost all of it goes to principal.

Lesson 2 Free

A worked example, computed step by step

Here is a diminishing-balance loan, the kind where interest is computed on what you still owe.

Example only, not a real offer. Borrow ₱50,000 at 1.5% per month, diminishing balance, for 12 months. The fixed payment formula is P × r / (1 − (1 + r)−n) = 50,000 × 0.015 / (1 − 1.015−12) = ₱4,584.00 per month. Month 1: interest = 50,000 × 1.5% = ₱750.00, principal = ₱3,834.00, balance ₱46,166.00. Month 2: interest = ₱692.49, principal = ₱3,891.51, balance ₱42,274.49. Month 12: interest ₱67.74, principal ₱4,516.26, balance ₱0. Total paid ₱55,008.00, total interest ₱5,008.00.

Lesson 3 Free

Add-on vs diminishing, same number, different cost

Some loans quote an add-on or flat rate. Interest is computed on the original principal for every month, even after you have paid part of it. So the same stated rate costs more.

Example only, not a real offer. Same ₱50,000, 1.5% per month, 12 months, but add-on: interest = 50,000 × 1.5% × 12 = ₱9,000. Monthly = 59,000 / 12 = ₱4,916.67. That is ₱3,992 more interest than the diminishing example above, for the same "1.5%".

4

Lesson 4 Free

How to use this when you shop for a loan

Ask three things: Is the rate add-on or diminishing? What is the total amount I will pay including fees? Can I get the full schedule in writing? The Truth in Lending Act (RA 3765) requires creditors to give a clear written statement of finance charges before the transaction is completed.

Then compare loans by the total you pay, not by the monthly.

5

Lesson 5 Free

Paying early

On a diminishing loan, extra payments toward principal reduce future interest. Some lenders charge a prepayment fee, so ask first and get the answer in writing.

6

Lesson 6 Free

Reading an amortization schedule

A schedule is a table with each month's payment split into interest and principal, and the balance after. If the lender gives you one, check that the interest falls each month on a diminishing loan. If it stays the same every month, it is likely add-on.

FAQ

Is a longer term always worse?

Not always. A lower monthly can protect your budget. But the total interest is usually higher. Choose the shortest term you can comfortably pay.

Why is my first payment mostly interest?

Because interest is computed on the full balance at the start. As the balance drops, so does the interest part.

Does the calculator include fees?

No. It shows interest only. Add fees from your disclosure statement to get the full cost.
