The 50-30-20 rule for Filipino salaries, made realistic

50% needs, 30% wants, 20% ipon. Pero paano kung Metro Manila rent pa lang, kalahati na?

50-30-20 is a starting point, not a law. Here is how to use it on a Philippine salary, adjust it honestly, and still save something every payday.

Lesson 1 Free

What the three buckets mean

Needs (50%): rent, food, utilities, transport, minimum loan payments, family support you are committed to. Wants (30%): eating out, shopping, subscriptions, gimik. Savings and debt (20%): emergency fund, goals, extra payments to clear debt faster.

Example only, not a real offer. Take-home pay ₱25,000 a month: needs ₱12,500, wants ₱7,500, savings and debt ₱5,000.

Lesson 2 Free

Use take-home pay, not gross

Your payslip deductions like tax, SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG are already gone before money reaches you. Budget from what actually lands in your account.

Lesson 3 Free

When needs are more than 50%

That is common, especially in cities. Try 60-20-20 or even 70-20-10 for now. The important part is that the savings bucket is never zero. Then work on the biggest need, like moving closer to work or sharing rent, over time.

Example only. Bea in Quezon City takes home ₱25,000, and needs are ₱16,000, more than half. She uses 64-26-10: wants ₱6,500, savings ₱2,500. Once her motor loan is paid, she moves to 54-26-20.

4

Lesson 4 Free

Where family support goes

If padala is fixed and expected, put it in needs. If it is extra and flexible, put it in wants. Being honest about this keeps the budget from breaking every month.

5

Lesson 5 Free

Make it automatic

On payday, move the savings bucket first to a separate account. What you do not see, you will not spend. For where to keep it, see our guide on bank vs digital bank vs e-wallet.

6

Lesson 6 Free

Review every three months

Your salary, rent and family needs change. Every quarter, look at the last three months and adjust the split. If wants keep going over, that is useful information, not a failure. Either reduce them or accept a smaller savings rate for now while you fix needs.

7

Lesson 7 Free

For couples and households

Couples can use one combined 50-30-20, or each person keeps their own and shares agreed needs. What matters is that everyone knows the plan and the savings bucket exists.

Example only. Mark and Liza in Cagayan de Oro combine take-home pay of ₱45,000. Needs ₱24,000, wants ₱12,000, savings ₱9,000. Each gets ₱3,000 personal allowance from wants, no questions asked.

Your 7-day action

  1. Run your take-home pay in the calculator.
  2. Set up an automatic transfer for savings on payday.
  3. Review after one month.

FAQ

What if I am paid weekly or daily?

Apply the same split to each payout, or to your average monthly income.

Should debt payments be in needs or savings?

Minimum payments go in needs. Extra payments to clear debt faster go in the 20%.

Is 20% too ambitious?

Start with any amount, even 5%. The habit matters more than the number at first.
