Lesson 1 Free

Know your number and your reason

Write down exactly what the money is for and the smallest amount that solves it. Borrowing "extra just in case" means paying interest on money you did not need.

2

Lesson 2 Free

Do the affordability math at home

List your monthly income and essentials. Whatever is left must cover the new payment and still leave room for a small savings amount. If it only fits on paper when everything goes perfectly, it does not fit.

Example only, not a real offer. Mae earns ₱22,000 take-home in Davao. Essentials are ₱16,000. Left: ₱6,000. A ₱5,500 monthly payment leaves only ₱500, too thin. A ₱2,500 payment leaves room to keep saving.

3

Lesson 3 Free

Check the lender

For lending and financing companies, confirm they have SEC authority to operate (RA 9474). For banks, confirm they are BSP-supervised. Look up the exact company name, not just the app name.

4

Lesson 4 Free

Protect your privacy

Be careful with apps that ask for access to your contact list or photos. Grant only what is needed for the loan. If an app will not work without full contact access, consider that a reason to walk away.

5

Lesson 5 Free

Get it in writing

Ask for the disclosure statement, the full schedule and all fees before you accept. The Truth in Lending Act (RA 3765) requires written disclosure of finance charges before the deal is completed.

6

Lesson 6 Free

Plan the payments, then set reminders

Put every due date in your phone calendar, a few days early. Pay from the same account each time and keep screenshots or receipts. If something goes wrong, contact the lender early, before the due date.

7

Lesson 7 Free

After you get the money

Use it only for the stated purpose. Keep all receipts and the loan documents in one folder or cloud album. When you finish paying, request a certificate of full payment and keep it. It helps if records ever need correcting.

The 10-point checklist

  1. I know the exact purpose and amount.
  2. The payment fits after essentials and a little savings.
  3. I know the total amount I will pay.
  4. I know if the rate is add-on or diminishing.
  5. I have the list of all fees.
  6. The lender is registered and authorized.
  7. I did not give unnecessary phone permissions.
  8. I have the disclosure statement and schedule.
  9. Due dates are in my calendar.
  10. I have a plan if income drops.

FAQ

Does a small first loan help my credit history?

Paying any loan on time builds a positive record with lenders that report to the CIC. Borrow only if you actually need it.

Someone asked me to pay a fee before the loan is released.

That is a common scam pattern. Stop and verify through the lender's official channels.

Can I cancel after approval?

It depends on the lender. Ask before you accept the money.
