Rainy day emergency fund: how much is enough?

Hindi mo kailangan ng milyon. Kailangan mo ng target na totoo para sa buhay mo.

An emergency fund is money set aside for the unplanned: job loss, illness, typhoon repairs. Here is how to find your number and reach it step by step.

1

Lesson 1 Free

Count essentials, not your whole spending

Add up one month of true essentials: rent, food, utilities, transport, minimum debt payments, medicines and committed family support. That is your monthly essentials number.

2

Lesson 2 Free

Choose your months

A common starting guide is 3 months of essentials for stable salaried work, and closer to 6 months if your income is irregular, you are the only earner, or you have dependents. These are guidelines, not rules. For daily earners, see our guide on building a fund on daily income.

Example only. Monthly essentials ₱18,000. Three months: ₱54,000. Six months: ₱108,000.

3

Lesson 3 Free

Break it into rungs

A big target can feel impossible. Start with one week of essentials, then one month, then three. Celebrate each rung.

4

Lesson 4 Free

Where to keep it

Keep it separate from spending money, easy to withdraw, and in an account insured by PDIC where applicable. See our guides on bank vs digital bank vs e-wallet and where to park idle money for current rates and the rules we checked.

5

Lesson 5 Free

What counts as an emergency

Unplanned, necessary and urgent. A sale on a phone is not. If you use the fund, refilling it becomes the next savings goal.

6

Lesson 6 Free

Speed it up without stress

Automate a fixed amount every payday, even small. Add windfalls like part of a bonus or a tax refund. When a loan is fully paid, keep "paying" the same amount into your fund instead. You are already used to living without that money.

Example only. Ella in Cavite just finished a ₱2,000 monthly gadget installment. She now moves ₱2,000 to her emergency fund every month. In a year that adds ₱24,000.

7

Lesson 7 Free

Insurance and the fund

Your fund handles small and medium shocks. Bigger risks like serious illness may need other protection. Learn what your existing benefits already cover, like PhilHealth or company HMO, before deciding anything else. This guide does not recommend any product.

FAQ

Should I pay debt or build the fund first?

Many people build a small starter fund first, then focus on high-interest debt, then grow the fund.

Can I keep it in an e-wallet?

An e-wallet balance is not a bank deposit. Savings products inside some apps are bank accounts. Read our bank vs e-wallet guide.

Is cash at home OK?

A small amount for typhoon days is practical. Keep most of it in an account so it is safer.
