Principal, interest, term

Principal is the amount borrowed. Interest is the price of using it. Term or tenor is how long you have to pay. These three decide most of the cost.

Example Si Carlo, a call center agent in Pasig, sees "₱30,000, 24 months". The ₱30,000 is principal, 24 months is the term. He still needs the rate and the type of rate. Example only, not a real offer.

Add-on rate vs diminishing balance

Add-on (flat): interest on the original amount every month. Diminishing balance: interest on what is left. Same number, add-on costs more. See the worked example in Amortization simplified.

Effective interest rate

The effective rate tries to show the true yearly cost after accounting for how and when you pay, and sometimes fees. It is the best single number for comparing offers. If the lender does not show it, ask for the total amount payable instead and compare that.

Fees you might see

  • Processing or service fee: sometimes deducted from the loan, so you receive less than the principal.
  • Documentary stamp tax and notarial fees on some loans.
  • Late payment penalty: charged after the due date.
  • Prepayment fee: for paying early, on some loans.

Ask for each in writing. Under the Truth in Lending Act (RA 3765), creditors must give a clear written statement of the finance charges before the deal is closed.

Example only, not a real offer. Approved ₱20,000 with a ₱1,000 processing fee deducted upfront. You receive ₱19,000 but repay interest on ₱20,000. Your real cost is higher than the stated rate.

Collateral, co-maker, guarantor

Collateral is an asset you pledge, like a car title. Co-maker is a person who signs and becomes liable if you do not pay. Being a co-maker for a friend is a real obligation. Only sign if you could pay the whole thing yourself.

Grace period, due date, default

Due date is the last day to pay. Some loans give a short grace period. Default means you failed to pay as agreed, which can trigger penalties, collection and a negative credit record.

7

Restructuring and refinancing

Restructuring changes the terms of an existing loan, often a longer term or lower monthly, usually when you are struggling to pay. Refinancing means taking a new loan to pay off the old one. Both can help, but compare the new total cost against the old.

FAQ

What should I ask for before signing?

The written disclosure statement, the full payment schedule, and a list of all fees.

Is a co-maker just a reference?

No. A co-maker can be required to pay. A character reference is different.

What if I already signed and do not understand it?

Ask the lender to walk you through the schedule. Keep copies of everything you sign and pay.
