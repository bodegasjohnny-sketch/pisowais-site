Debt snowball vs avalanche for Filipino households

Pick one method, follow it every month, and watch the list get shorter.

Educational only, not financial advice. If debt is overwhelming, talk to your bank about restructuring before you miss payments.

Most households with debt do not have one loan. They have a credit card, a salary loan, an appliance installment, maybe a loan from a relative. Paying a little on everything feels responsible, but it often keeps you in debt longer. A payoff method gives every extra peso a job.

Step zero: list everything

Write every debt with four numbers: balance, interest rate, minimum payment and due date. Include informal utang. Seeing it all on one page is uncomfortable, but it is the start of control.

Then stop adding new debt. If you have credit cards, pause spending on them until you are paying statements in full again.

The avalanche method

Pay the minimum on every debt. Put every extra peso on the debt with the highest interest rate. When it is gone, roll that payment into the next highest rate.

Why: it saves the most interest. Credit cards in the Philippines can charge up to 3% a month (36% a year) under the BSP ceiling in Circular No. 1165, so they are usually first in line.

Best for: people who are motivated by math and can stay patient if the first debt takes a while.

The snowball method

Pay the minimum on every debt. Put every extra peso on the debt with the smallest balance. When it is gone, roll that payment into the next smallest.

Why: quick wins. Clearing a whole debt in the first month or two builds momentum, and fewer bills mean fewer chances to miss one.

Best for: people who have tried before and lost motivation.

A worked example

A household has PHP 5,000 a month extra for debt and these balances (illustrative numbers):

  • Credit card A: PHP 60,000 at 3% per month
  • Appliance installment: PHP 12,000 at 0%, PHP 2,000 per month left
  • Salary loan: PHP 40,000 at a lower fixed rate
  • Loan from a relative: PHP 8,000, no interest

Avalanche order: credit card A first, then the salary loan, then the 0% items. This cuts the most interest because the card is by far the most expensive.

Snowball order: relative's loan first (gone in under 2 months), then the appliance, then the salary loan, then the card. More early wins, but the card keeps charging up to 3% a month in the meantime.

A practical hybrid: many households clear one tiny debt first for motivation, then switch to avalanche. And whatever you choose, never let a 0% installment lapse into a penalty.

Rules that apply to both

  1. Never miss a minimum payment. Late fees and a damaged credit record make everything harder.
  2. Keep a small emergency buffer, even PHP 5,000 to 10,000, so a surprise does not go back on the card.
  3. Use windfalls, like 13th month pay or a bonus, for the target debt.
  4. Do not borrow from informal lenders to pay cards. The effective cost is usually far higher.
  5. Ask your bank about restructuring or a balance transfer if the payment is impossible. Read the fees.

What about family utang?

It often has no interest but carries relationship cost. Agree on a schedule in writing, even a simple message, and pay on time. Treat it as seriously as a bank loan.

Track progress where you can see it

Write your total debt at the top of a page and update it on the same day each month. Some households stick the list on the refrigerator. Seeing the number fall is the best motivation either method can give you.

After the last debt

Keep paying the same amount, but to yourself: first a full emergency fund in an insured savings account, then long-term goals. The habit you built is now your wealth engine.
